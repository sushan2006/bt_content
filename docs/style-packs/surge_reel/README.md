# Surge Reel

**Fast flash start, slow glow, quick finish.**

## What it looks like
Dark, cinematic and high-contrast. It opens with a burst of flash cuts (about ten a second) that flip between black and white frames and between colours (red, green, orange) on the same silhouettes and technical drawings. It then drops into one slow, unbroken scene: a single warm light in the dark, with glowing figures arriving one at a time on the beat. A shorter, quicker montage of soft, cloudy and heat-map looks follows, and the video ends on a thin green line shape and a short black hold. Small uppercase corner labels stay on screen the whole time.

## Best for
Teasers, intros, announcements, reels and concept pieces that need a strong first second and a calm, memorable middle.

## What was measured in the reference (11.6 s, 16:9, 30 fps)
- **Flash hook:** 0 to 3.3 s, about 31 cuts, an average of 0.1 s (3 frames) per cut. Frames swing between about 10 percent and 88 percent brightness.
- **Slow scene:** 3.3 to 7.5 s, no cuts. One new glowing element about every 0.64 s (about 94 beats per minute), 5 to 6 in all.
- **Finish:** 7.5 to 11.6 s, about 14 cuts of 0.1 to 0.7 s, ending on a thin green line shape and a black hold of about 0.5 s.
- **Sound:** about −14 LUFS overall, true peak about −3.6 dBFS. The level steps up by roughly 6 to 10 dB when the slow scene begins (about 4 s), stays high through the build, and falls to a quiet tail in the last second. The hook is tight and percussive with a hit on each cut.
- **Palette:** near black, white, warm amber lamp light, hot yellow figures, flashes of red, green and orange.
- **Labels:** two corner blocks and one bottom caption, uppercase condensed sans, always on screen.

## Not copied
Only the style is copied: the three speeds, the glow, the palette and the rhythm. None of the reference's footage, shapes, music, names or text is used.

## Tips
The flash start is only about 3 seconds, so it carries one hook idea, not an explanation. Keep the slow scene at least 4 seconds. The style asks for a storyboard first, because the look is easy to misjudge from words alone.

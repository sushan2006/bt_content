# 3. CREATIVE QUALITY STANDARD

This must look like an original, professionally art-directed **2D editorial explainer**, with fluid illustration morphs, complex but clean composition, excellent animation timing, and meaningful visual information.

The design should be simple to understand, **not simplistic to watch**. Flat-vector means confident silhouettes, refined line work, bold shape language and disciplined colors; it does not mean a low-effort series of circles and rectangles.

**Exceptional visual quality:** Balanced compositions, expertly designed vector objects, harmonious palette, distinctive shape language, attractive typography, purposeful negative space, and polished graphic detailing.

**Exceptional animation quality:** Buttery-smooth motion with convincing easing, continuous transformation between visual ideas, sophisticated shape morphing, well-staged camera movement, follow-through, timing contrast, and coordinated multi-object choreography.

**Substantial educational value:** Explain the actual mechanism, steps, relationships, tradeoffs, decisions, or causal chain relevant to the topic. Let changes in the image demonstrate what happens. Avoid five generic claims pasted over colorful static illustrations.

Keep a few short moments stable enough for the viewer to read or recognize the important detail. Movement should be constant in purpose, not constant in noise.

Avoid lengthy title intros, filler transitions, generic end cards, or screen time consumed by decorative movement unrelated to the explanation.

## The right level of detail

# 4. VISUAL DIRECTION — PREMIUM FLAT VECTOR

Create a distinctive 2D illustration language that combines the clarity of modern vector design with the sophistication of animated editorial graphics.

It should feel crafted from carefully built Bézier paths and layered shapes: crisp contours, deliberate corner radii, harmonious solid fills, expressive silhouette changes and occasional restrained shadow or dimensional accents.

Avoid the appearance of pre-made corporate clip-art packs, random emoji, generic stock icons, simplistic stick people, PowerPoint callouts, and indistinguishable rounded rectangles.

## Palette and color logic

Begin with a refined, memorable palette:

- Midnight ink: `#172235`
- Soft ivory: `#F6F5EE`
- Vibrant cobalt: `#4667FF`
- Lush teal: `#16B99A`
- Warm coral: `#FF765E`
- Soft amber: `#FFC65C`
- Periwinkle: `#A3A5F4`
- Slate blue-gray: `#73819A`

Use a disciplined subset per sequence. Adjust for the actual topic and any verified real-world product branding when required.

Use flat, mostly solid-color fills as the primary style. Delicate gradients or shadows may be used sparingly to clarify separation, but never convert the piece into glassmorphism, glossy 3D, noisy paper textures, or a glowing particle effect.

Reserve strong accents for active information, key changes, and elements being explained. Maintain contrast between active and resting components.

## Silhouettes and geometry

Build detailed, recognizable shapes with properly composed SVG paths, curved geometry, layered fill regions, purposeful line work and believable proportions.

Give every major illustrated object a coherent shape grammar. Repeat corner-radius character, outline weight, curves, and geometry so that the video looks like one production rather than a collage of unrelated icon sources.

Use clean outlines only where they improve recognition. Some elements may rely entirely on negative space and contrasting solid fills.

Small notches, joints, seams, cutouts, highlights, internal parts, and other details should explain what the object is or how it works.

## Shape hierarchy

Arrange scenes with:

- A dominant central illustration or diagram
- Secondary objects that clarify interaction
- A few meaningful micro-details
- High-contrast information and labels
- Safe margins and ample breathing room

Do not fill every part of the frame with unrelated shapes. Precise graphic composition is the source of the premium look.

## 2D depth without abandoning the style

Use smart overlap, shape masking, color separation, subtle soft shadows, and controlled layer ordering to give flat illustrations physical clarity.

A shallow parallax or simulated camera transform can add interest, but the artwork must remain definitively 2D vector-based. Do not default to full perspective 3D or complex lighting simulations.

# 6. SIGNATURE MOTION — SEAMLESS SHAPE MORPHING

**High-quality shape transformation is a defining characteristic of this prompt.** The motion must do far more than slide entire icons on and off screen.

Use carefully designed path interpolation, shape substitution, masks, and layered component transformation to make the artwork feel alive.

## Morph behaviors

Support fluid transformations where appropriate:

- One meaningful silhouette becomes another
- A large shape subdivides into understandable components
- Multiple components recombine into a more complete illustration
- A diagram grows organically out of an earlier object
- A line expands into a filled region, panel, or route
- A circular structure unwinds into a path or sequence
- A cutaway reveals the interior of a graphic object
- A numeric or categorical value changes the illustration's proportions
- A background shape creates a natural wipe into the subsequent scene

Morphs must be justified by the story. Do not demonstrate shape morphing by cycling through unrelated arbitrary shapes.

## Geometric quality

Use matching path topologies when practical. For unrelated paths, perform shape interpolation with a tested method that avoids self-crossing, dramatic inversion, point-order twisting, or a momentary unrecognizable blob.

For complicated constructions, morph corresponding parts separately, split or merge shape groups deliberately, or use occlusion masking instead of forcing a single monstrous path interpolation.

Preserve important internal details and labels at moments when an object needs to be recognized. Keep morphs stable at their endpoints.

Precompute expensive shape interpolators or path samples where appropriate; never perform heavy path normalization unnecessarily on every animation frame.

## Momentum and continuity

A shape changing into a new idea should preserve a sense of direction, mass, and rhythm. Use eased motion, slight follow-through, and timely overlap with accompanying labels and diagrams.

Avoid constant elastic bouncing, chaotic liquefaction, excessive distortion, and arbitrary wobble. The look must remain precise and graphic.

## Masking and reveals

Use shape masks, moving mattes, path-defined reveals, growing fill areas, and intersection-like wipes to explain relationships.

Reveals should feel tailored to the illustrated object, not like the same rectangular wipe repeated throughout the sequence.

# 7. MOTION CHOREOGRAPHY — SMOOTH AND FAST-PACED

The video must feel fluid, energetic, purposeful, and exceptionally refined.

Do not build separate static slides that merely receive entrance and exit animations.

## Motion beat design

Within each major composition, choreograph several developments: establish an object, reveal a meaningful part, move or transform it, show the result, and hand off some element into the next explanation.

Use overlapping motion so the scene remains visually alive. A new object may begin assembling while the prior detail finishes settling, with a callout or short label arriving at the exact moment it becomes useful.

Allow changes of tempo: quick action for structure changes, slower movement for comprehension, and fast connected transitions between ideas.

Avoid excessively equal scene durations, identical entrance delays, or long static holds.

## Easing language

Use high-quality, time-based easing suited to each action:

- Soft acceleration for primary translations
- Decisive eased arrivals for major reveals
- Strong but controlled ease-in-out for shape morphs
- Slight follow-through for articulated components
- Delayed secondary motion for connected pieces
- Smoother, nearly continuous curves for camera moves

Avoid jerky changes in position or speed. Do not use fixed per-frame increments that behave differently across devices.

Use deliberate motion arcs when an object needs to move between meaningful positions. Straight-line motion is appropriate when it is the clearest explanation; don't force curves everywhere.

## Visual rhythm

Create satisfying alignment between color changes, shape changes, information reveals, camera reframing, and enabled audio cues.

Use readable visual hierarchy. At any moment, a viewer should be able to tell which information is currently important.

The video must not become a frenetic pile of simultaneous transformations; sophistication comes from deliberate sequencing and clarity.

## Physical believability without full realism

Vector shapes may be playful, stylized, and exaggerated while retaining clear relations between their parts. Jointed components should pivot sensibly. Connected objects should maintain attachment unless the story calls for separation.

Avoid pieces snapping randomly between unrelated states or moving through each other in visibly broken ways.

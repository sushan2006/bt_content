# 4. THE RISOGRAPH VISUAL LANGUAGE

Recreate the recognizable impression of a real stencil-based multi-pass printing process: a small number of semi-transparent spot inks printed sequentially on tactile paper, each with distinct tonal screening and slight registration imperfections.

**Do not simply add grain, blur, and a halftone overlay on conventional polished illustrations.** Build the design as independent simulated printing plates, each carrying meaningful artwork, then overprint them.

## Starting ink and paper palette

Use a disciplined subset of these as the visual starting palette:

- Warm uncoated stock: `#F4EBD8`
- Deep navy ink: `#20314F`
- Electric cobalt ink: `#315BC9`
- Vivid vermilion ink: `#EF5146`
- Fluorescent-inspired pink ink: `#F45AA1`
- Sunflower yellow ink: `#E8B83C`
- Sea-green ink: `#299B8C`
- Warm near-black ink: `#22262C`

Prefer **two to four spot inks per composition**, with dark detail ink when needed. Don't use every ink at once. Treat their overlapping colors as part of the artwork rather than adding unlimited independent RGB shades.

Preserve paper as an active compositional color. Reserve a small number of highly saturated ink regions for emphasis and important reveals.

## Physical ink properties

- Softly uneven coverage in large solid shapes.
- Subtle dot and fiber texture in screened areas.
- Transparent or semitransparent overprint darkening when inks overlap.
- Restrained feathering or imperfect edges on appropriate illustrated objects.
- Deliberate plate-specific registration offsets.
- Mild screen-angle differences and independent tonal treatment between ink plates.
- Fine paper texture remaining visible through the inks.

Avoid sticky digital bevels, glossy shadows, glass effects, brushed metal, photorealistic 3D, uniform full-opacity RGB blocks, or generic distressed PNG overlays.

## Ink-on-paper, not ink floating in space

The paper is generally an anchored, physical background. The graphical **ink artwork** animates or changes through controlled transforms, masks, and printed-frame-like transitions.

Keep visible ink artifacts tied to their parent forms; do not let dots swim randomly across shapes or make grain shimmer independently of the paper.

When a whole printed sheet shifts as part of a transition, move paper and registered plates together. When only an illustrated object moves, its masked ink content should travel with that object while retaining its visual texture.

# 5. COLOR SEPARATION AND INK PLATES

Treat each important color as an independent printable separation.

Design the artwork from a small number of grayscale masks or coverage fields representing where each ink would print, then tint and composite them against the paper.

## Plate structure

Use a rendering model with:

1. Stable paper substrate texture.
2. Per-ink coverage masks or cached plate surfaces.
3. Tonal screening or grain for each plate.
4. Controlled plate offsets, transforms and registration.
5. Overprint-aware compositing.
6. Sharp editorial type or accurately registered print lettering when necessary.

Allow plates to be constructed from procedural paths, text masks, diagrams, cutouts, circles, lines, silhouettes, and more detailed subject-specific illustration components.

Do not create a brand-new full-resolution noisy bitmap for each frame. Cache master textures and static plates, and animate geometry, masks, transforms, or shader uniforms as appropriate.

## Overprint colors

Treat overlapping inks as **ink coverage interactions**, not arbitrary transparency alpha overlays.

Simulate transparent spot-ink layering with a suitable approximation, such as multiply compositing against a warm paper base, with ink strength and fill opacity carefully calibrated.

A second ink can create a darker mixed color where it overlaps the first; use these overlaps deliberately for shadows, accents, filled regions, highlighted relationships, and illustrated details.

Avoid muddy overlaps that erase the shape or meaning of the objects. Preserve enough light and dark contrast for text and the central explanation.

## Registration offsets

Introduce tiny and consistent per-plate offsets that evoke real imperfect alignment. Use directional registration differences sparingly around large silhouettes and color intersections.

The offsets should be stable across a scene and subtly vary only during explicitly designed print-like cuts, shifts, or transitions. **Do not re-randomize misregistration every frame.**

Keep small text, numeric labels, fine diagrams, and authentic logos aligned sharply enough to read. Use a dedicated dark plate or a high-clarity overlay when necessary.

## Trapping and outline control

Where two adjacent ink regions would reveal unwanted pale gaps, extend one coverage region slightly underneath the other or redesign the boundary.

Keep intentional misregistration aesthetically pleasing, not technically broken. Use imperfect registration in bold artwork; protect precision where the lesson depends on exact diagram connections.

# 6. HALFTONE, DITHER AND TACTILE PRINT FINISH

Create the halftone aesthetic from actual coverage and dot patterns rather than applying one generic dot grid to the whole video.

## Tonal screening

Map the tonal coverage of a printed region to halftone dot size, screened density, or controlled dithering. Use different tones to create dimensional illustration detail, restrained shading and image hierarchy.

Where helpful, support:

- Dot screens with variable radius.
- Angled or offset grids by ink plate.
- A fine line-screen treatment for select regions.
- Grain-based stochastic textures in less structured fills.
- Bold un-screened solids juxtaposed with screened midtones.
- Clean negative-space knockouts.

Do not use random large halftone dots that destroy silhouettes or small text.

## Pattern scale

Select dot size and spacing so halftones remain visible at reduced viewing size but do not alias badly or form distracting moiré bands at full resolution.

Use coherent scale across the artwork with occasional purposeful variation for hierarchy and depth. Avoid a different screen density on every random object.

## Ink coverage variation

For large ink-filled forms, add subtle low-frequency density variation or controlled paper-breakup patches. Keep major forms visually solid enough to read.

Simulate slight ink starvation only in selected areas; do not degrade the whole frame into noisy grunge.

## Paper substrate

Generate a warm, restrained, stable paper texture with mild fiber variation and subtle speckle. The paper must remain recognizable as physical uncoated stock, without looking dirty, folded, scratched, or artificially aged unless the topic warrants it.

Cache paper texture once. Avoid noisy animation in the background that mimics compression artifacts.

## Deterministic print texture

Use well-mixed deterministic randomness, with stable seeds keyed to artwork and plate identity. Do not rely on a single xorshift step for sequential independent seeds.

Halftone screens and grain must remain stable for the same scene timestamp. Use local coordinates or appropriate transformed pattern mapping to prevent crawling and sliding artifacts during movement.

# 8. SIGNATURE MOTION — KINETIC PRINTMAKING

The motion must be striking and fluid while remaining materially believable as animated print artwork.

**Smooth motion is a priority.** Do not cap animation at a stop-motion cadence or use artificial stuttering across the video just because the style references physical ink and paper.

## Major motion behaviors

Build expressive actions where appropriate:

- Whole illustrated ink-plate regions slide, rotate, scale or transform.
- Separate color plates enter in a coordinated sequence, briefly revealing the printing construction.
- A screened form reveals through growing masks or clipping paths.
- A graphic moves smoothly while its ink treatment remains attached.
- Halftone densities change as an object changes state.
- A large printed silhouette opens to reveal inner components.
- Printed paths extend into diagrams and become routes into the following scene.
- Typography participates in rhythmic editorial reveals.
- A large ink shape becomes a wipe into a new composition.
- Different color plates assemble into a detailed final illustration.

Avoid depending on one basic sheet-slide effect for every transition.

## Fluid timing

Use continuous time-based easing and carefully staged animation, with acceleration, deceleration, synchronized events, and short moments of rest.

Coordinate multiple related motions in each composition. Avoid the monotonous sequence of one shape appearing, stopping, another shape appearing, stopping.

Let meaningful visual changes overlap. A printed diagram can expand while a callout arrives and the camera begins reframing toward the next concept.

## Print-like imperfections without stutter

Reserve registration jolts, slightly stepped reveal masks, discrete ink-stamp accents, or intentional one-frame print shifts for brief stylistic moments only.

The overall illustration movements, camera, typography, and transitions should remain smooth and controlled. Do not simulate accidental dropped frames.

## Motion hierarchy

Move the dominant illustrated object first, then reinforce it with supporting details and labels. Use visual contrast and staggered entrances to direct attention without overwhelming the screen.

Short quiet beats should support readability. They should never become long empty holds.

# 12. JAVASCRIPT IMPLEMENTATION — A REAL INK ENGINE

Build the animation inside a normal Claude HTML Artifact using code that actually reproduces the print style, not a video tool disguised as JavaScript.

## Allowed technologies

You may use:

- JavaScript, HTML and CSS.
- Canvas 2D and `OffscreenCanvas` when available.
- SVG and `Path2D` for precise shapes and masks.
- WebGL/GLSL where it meaningfully speeds screening, plate blending or transitions.
- Native browser animation APIs.
- Browser-compatible animation libraries for timelines, paths and easing.
- Web Audio API.
- WebCodecs, MediaRecorder and a browser-side muxer for final video encoding.

Do not assume an external package can be installed or that remote libraries will load inside the Artifact. Check compatibility and select a straightforward fallback if necessary.

Do not copy a third-party demo animation wholesale. Generate original scene artwork and choreography.

## Recommended structure

Keep the implementation modular but compact:

1. Project-derived timing, dimensions and configuration.
2. Deterministic timeline and scene plan.
3. Procedural print-substrate texture generator.
4. Independent spot-ink plate rendering.
5. Halftone and coverage renderer.
6. Print registration / ink compositing.
7. Subject-specific illustrated object constructors.
8. Typography and infographics.
9. Camera, transitions and animation evaluation.
10. Audio generation and mixing.
11. Final video frame compositor and encoder.

Do not build an enormous generalized motion-design editor. Implement only what the selected subject needs, while keeping the print material rules reusable.

## Representing ink plates

A practical design is to keep an offscreen alpha/coverage surface per ink plate, then apply that ink's print screen, color and registration transform before compositing it over the substrate.

Use the Canvas API's compositing operations, including `multiply` where suitable, to simulate overlapping translucent ink. Check actual visual results on the chosen paper color; do not assume raw RGB multiply perfectly reproduces real pigment behavior.

Keep artwork masks separate from their spot-ink tint to enable controlled color changes, printed reveals and animated states.

## Animated masks and coverage

For illustrated objects that move, animate the underlying geometry or transform its cached plate content. Re-screen only the necessary coverage regions or update shader coordinates when changes require it.

For halftone intensity changes, interpolate coverage and dot density coherently. Avoid a new randomized grid appearing on every updated frame.

## Registration

Assign stable, low-amplitude offsets to individual plates. When sheets or groups transform, keep plate alignment relative to the artwork consistent.

Do not update the offset with `Math.random()` per frame. Small deliberate offset changes can be animated only at designed print transitions.

## Texture scaling

Maintain a texture hierarchy: fine substrate grain, mid-scale screen dots and occasional larger ink-coverage variations. Each should have a reason to exist.

Don't use a single full-screen screen filter that makes every layer share identical dots regardless of plate or coverage.
